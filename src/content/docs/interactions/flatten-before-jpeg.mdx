`rotate` by a non-right angle exposes new corners, transparent unless you give a `background`. JPEG cannot store transparency, so what those corners become depends on whether you flattened **after** the rotation.

<Example
  id="order.flatten"
  results={[
    { file: 'rotate-then-flatten.jpg', label: 'rotate → flatten', alt: 'The emblem rotated 30 degrees and then flattened onto paper: the corners are paper-coloured.' },
    { file: 'flatten-then-rotate.jpg', label: 'flatten → rotate', alt: 'The emblem flattened first and then rotated: the new corners are black.' },
  ]}
/>

- **rotate → flatten**: the flatten sees the transparent corners and paints them with the paper colour.
- **flatten → rotate**: the emblem had no transparency left to flatten, then `rotate` created new transparent corners that nothing resolves, and the JPEG encoder wrote them **black** (observed with libvips 8.18).

The rule is the one in [Encoders](/oss/hokusai/encoding-output/formats#alpha): flatten **last**, immediately before a format without alpha. Alternatively give `rotate` the final background colour (`rotate(by:background:)`), which fills the corners during the rotation.
