A layer is placed in the coordinates of the pipeline **at the moment of `composite`**, so anything that changes the size afterwards changes the layer too.

## Resize and composite \{#resize}
<Example
  id="order.composite"
  results={[

    { file: 'resize-then-composite.webp', label: 'resize → composite', alt: 'The landscape at 400 pixels wide with a large emblem: the logo kept its 200-pixel size.' },
    { file: 'blur-all.webp', label: 'composite → resize → blur', alt: 'The whole picture blurred, including the emblem.' },
    { file: 'blur-photo-only.webp', label: 'blur the photo, then composite', alt: 'A blurred landscape with a sharp emblem on top.' },
  ]}
/>

- **composite → resize**: the logo at (40, 40), 200 px wide on a 1600-px photo, becomes 50 px on the 400-px result, placed at (10, 10).
- **resize → composite**: the same layer at (40, 40) on the 400-px photo covers half the width.
- **Blur**: effects after `composite` act on the logo too. To keep a logo crisp, apply the effect to the photo first and composite last, as in the fourth image (where the logo was also resized to suit).

## What stays the same \{#same}
Layers are drawn in array order, later over earlier ([Composition](/oss/hokusai/text-composition/composite#order)). A layer is a pipeline of its own, so its transformations (resize, blur, negate) happen *before* it is composed; the order of operations **inside** the layer is the same story as anywhere else.
