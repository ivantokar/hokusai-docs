Four operations change which pixels are kept or added without scaling them: `extract` cuts a rectangle, `extend` adds canvas, `trim` removes a uniform border, and `smartCrop` cuts a window around the most interesting region. The sample images are the 1600 × 1000 landscape and the 400 × 400 emblem (with transparent corners).

## extract \{#extract}
`extract(x:y:width:height:)` returns the rectangle whose top-left corner is `(x, y)`.

<Example
  id="geometry.extract"
  results={[
    { file: 'lighthouse.png', label: 'on the original', alt: 'A 400 by 560 vertical slice of the landscape containing the lighthouse.' },
    { file: 'lighthouse-after-resize.png', label: 'after resize', alt: 'A 200 by 280 slice taken after halving the image; it shows the same lighthouse at half size.' },
  ]}
/>

| Parameter | Type | Bounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `x`, `y` | `Int` | `0…10,000,000` |
| `width`, `height` | `Int` | `1…2,147,483,647` |

Coordinates count from the top-left of the image **as it is at that point of the pipeline**. The same numbers after a `resize` address different pixels, so scale them too (above, the second call halves every number). A rectangle that reaches outside the image is not clipped: it fails when the pipeline is evaluated (see [Limits](#limits)).

## extend \{#extend}
`extend` only adds canvas. It has three forms.

- `extend(to:anchor:background:)`: grow to an exact `ImageSize`, placing the image at `anchor` (a `Gravity`: `.center`, the four sides or the four corners) and filling with a colour. The default background is transparent.
- `extend(to:anchor:mode:)`: the same, filled according to an `ExtendMode`.
- `extend(top:left:bottom:right:mode:)`: add that many pixels on each side (`0…10,000,000`).

`ExtendMode` is `.background(Color)`, `.copy` (repeat the nearest edge pixel outwards), `.repeat` (tile the image) or `.mirror` (tile it, mirroring every other tile).

The new area is filled; the image's own pixels are not touched, so transparent parts of the original stay transparent even when the background is a colour. Fill them with [`flatten`](/oss/hokusai/channels-transparency/) if that is not what you want. To grow *and* scale to a fixed box, use `resize(fit: .contain)` instead.

## trim \{#trim}
`trim` removes a border of nearly uniform colour. `trim(threshold:)` is the short form; `trim(_ options: TrimOptions)` adds control:

| `TrimOptions` | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `threshold` | `10` | Tolerance for treating a pixel as background; lower removes only nearly identical borders. Must be finite and `≥ 0`. |
| `background` | `.topLeft` | The colour to remove: the top-left pixel at that point of the pipeline, or `.color(Color)` (grey or RGB images). |
| `lineArt` | `false` | Detect borders in line art and synthetic images by strong edges instead of colour difference. |
| `margin` | `0` | Pixels of background to keep around the content (clamped to the image). |

<Example
  id="geometry.trim"
  results={[

    { file: 'trimmed.png', label: 'trim()', alt: 'The emblem alone; the border is gone.' },
    { file: 'trimmed-margin.png', label: 'margin: 12', alt: 'The emblem with a 12-pixel margin of the white border kept.' },

The scan runs when the pipeline is evaluated, not when `trim` is called, and an image that is entirely background is left unchanged rather than cropped to nothing. Because the background is the **top-left pixel**, an image whose corner is part of the subject (such as the emblem's transparent corner) is trimmed by what that corner looks like; flatten onto a known colour first when in doubt.

## smartCrop \{#smart-crop}
`smartCrop(width:height:strategy:)` cuts a `width × height` window of the original pixels, without resizing, around the region chosen by the `strategy`: `.attention` (skin tones, saturated colour, detail; the default) or `.entropy` (most local variation).

<Example
  id="geometry.smart-crop"
  results={[
    { file: 'attention.png', label: '.attention', alt: 'A 500 by 500 window around the lighthouse and sun.' },
    { file: 'entropy.png', label: '.entropy', alt: 'A 500 by 500 window chosen by local variation.' },

To scale *and* crop in one step use `resize(fit: .cover, position: .attention)`, which makes the same selection ([Resize](/oss/hokusai/geometry/resize#position)). A window larger than the image is clamped to the image.

## Limits \{#limits}
<Example id="geometry.limits" />

| Call on a 400 × 400 image | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `extract(x: 100, y: 100, width: 200, height: 200)` | 200 × 200 |
| `extract(x: 300, y: 300, width: 200, height: 200)` | throws `HokusaiError.transform` (`extract`), when evaluated |
| `extend(to: 300 × 300)` | throws `HokusaiError.invalidOption` ("extend cannot reduce an image"), when evaluated |
| `smartCrop(width: 900, height: 900)` | 400 × 400: clamped |
| `trim()` with nothing to trim | 400 × 400: unchanged |

Option values outside their bounds (a negative `x`, a zero `width`, a negative `margin`) throw `HokusaiError.invalidOption` when the pipeline is built, before any image is opened.
